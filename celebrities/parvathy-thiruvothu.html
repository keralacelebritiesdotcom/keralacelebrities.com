Biography

A performer drawn to complex characters

Parvathy Thiruvothu entered Malayalam cinema in 2006 with Out of Syllabus. Her early work included Notebook and Vinodayathra, before she expanded into Kannada and Tamil cinema.

Her career gained a major new dimension through films such as Poo, Maryan, Bangalore Days, Ennu Ninte Moideen and Charlie. These performances established her as an actor associated with emotionally detailed and character-driven roles.

Take Off became a defining milestone. Her portrayal of Sameera earned the Kerala State Film Award for Best Actress, a National Film Award Special Mention and the IFFI Best Actor – Female award. citeturn1search0turn1search3

She followed that success with films including Koode, Uyare, Virus, Halal Love Story, Wonder Women, Puzhu and Ullozhukku, continuing to move between intimate dramas, social themes and mainstream projects.

At a glance

  • Born in Kozhikode, Kerala, in 1988.
  • Trained in Bharatanatyam before becoming known as an actor.
  • Worked across Malayalam, Tamil, Kannada and Hindi cinema.
  • Received two Kerala State Film Awards for Best Actress.
  • Earned a National Film Award Special Mention for Take Off.
  • Known for selecting women-centred and socially resonant characters.

Beyond the screen

An actor associated with thoughtful cinema

Parvathy's film choices have often combined strong character work with themes involving relationships, social pressures, gender and personal resilience. Her career has moved between mainstream Malayalam releases and more experimental or issue-driven projects.

She has also worked in Tamil, Kannada and Hindi productions, giving her career a broader South Indian and pan-Indian dimension. Her work in films such as Qarib Qarib Singlle, Thangalaan and the anthology series Navarasa reflects that range.
